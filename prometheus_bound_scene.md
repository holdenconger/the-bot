PROMETHEUS BOUND: NEW SCENE

DRAMATIS PERSONAE
PROMETHEUS, a Titan, bound to the rock
CHORUS OF OCEANIDS
HERMES, herald of Zeus

[The same desolate cliff by the sea. PROMETHEUS is still bound. The
CHORUS hovers in the air, their wings low with pity. The light dims,
as if a storm were gathering beyond the horizon.]

CHORUS:
    The wind grows sharp, the sea draws inward,
    as if it listens. The air is a mouth
    that swallows our song. What footstep
    climbs the rock? What word rides the thunder?

[A low rumble. HERMES enters swiftly, his staff bright against the
darkening sky. He does not look upon the CHORUS.]

HERMES:
    Prometheus, son of Iapetus, I speak
    the will of Zeus. The king commands once more:
    reveal the name that threatens his long reign,
    or learn the last measure of his wrath.

PROMETHEUS:
    Herald, you are swift, but your words are slow,
    old as the whip that taught you how to speak.
    I know the will you carry. I refuse it.

HERMES:
    Refuse, and ruin follows. The sky has room
    for your defiance only so far. A pit,
    lightless, without sea or voice, is readied.

PROMETHEUS:
    Then let it be readied. I will not trade
    my knowing for your master's brief favor.
    I have endured the first weight of his reign;
    I will endure the last.

CHORUS:
    [Softly] O mind unbent, O spirit nailed
    to stone, is there no middle path? Speak,
    not for his sake, but for ours who watch
    and tremble. The storm is our answer, too.

PROMETHEUS:
    Friends of the sea, your tears are salt,
    but salt does not unmake iron. I will not
    unmake myself to soothe a tyrant's fear.

HERMES:
    You call him tyrant. He calls you thief.
    You stole fire, you taught men craft and measure,
    and set them upright. Your gift is a chain
    that binds you here. Would you add words
    to make it heavier?

PROMETHEUS:
    I taught them to look up without terror.
    If that is theft, I do not repent.
    The chain is his, but the choice is mine.

[The rumble deepens. A harsh wind crosses the stage, lifting the
CHORUS as if to scatter them.]

CHORUS:
    The sea pulls back, the cliff is hollow,
    the sky is a drum. Speak, or suffer,
    or teach us how to endure without speech.

PROMETHEUS:
    Endure. That is the lesson the gods
    refuse to learn. They think power is breath,
    but it is time. Time is my ally.

HERMES:
    Time is no ally of the bound. You will beg.
    The eagle will return. The rock will drink.
    Your pride will shrink to a whisper in the dark.

PROMETHEUS:
    The eagle has fed. The rock has heard.
    Yet my silence stays. Tell Zeus this:
    his fear speaks through your mouth, not my own.

HERMES:
    I have delivered the warning. I go.
    Do not claim ignorance when the earth
    opens beneath you.

[HERMES exits into the storm. The CHORUS settles, shaken.]

CHORUS:
    The herald has gone, yet his words remain
    like smoke that will not lift. Tell us, Titan,
    why choose silence over the chance of peace?

PROMETHEUS:
    Because his peace is only quiet chains.
    Because the world must hear that a god
    can suffer and not yield. There is a law
    older than thunder: what is unjust
    must meet a voice that will not bend.

CHORUS:
    Then we will stay and sing beside you,
    though the cloud breaks and the cliff shudders.
    We will not flee the sound of it.

[A first heavy drop of rain strikes the rock. PROMETHEUS lifts his
face to it, unmoved.]

PROMETHEUS:
    Let the storm come. It cannot unmake
    what has already been forged in fire.
